### answer (answer) — How does TextOS know a draft answers its brief?

> Alongside its fact-check, TextOS runs a separate check that compares the draft with the brief it was written for. It looks at three things: each point the brief requires is either backed by a statement with a source or left out for a reason a person accepted, the sections the brief requires are present, and the target search query is addressed. A draft goes to preview only when both the fact-check and the brief check pass. Being right and being what you asked for are answered as two separate questions, instead of being blended into one score.

### section-1 (section) — Accurate is not the same as complete

> A fact-check reads the sentences a draft contains and asks whether a source supports them. It does not ask whether the sentences that should be there are there. TextOS's design record describes this failure plainly: a draft can be certified true while covering only one of the required points of its brief, an outcome it calls an empty draft certified true.

Such a draft can be accurate line by line and still leave the reader without the answer they came for. Keeping it as a test case means the gap between correct and complete is checked on purpose, not discovered by a disappointed reader.

### section-4 (section) — What this check does not tell you

> The target query counts as addressed when its key words appear in the rendered text of the draft. That is a presence test, not a judgment of how well the question is answered. ### verify (section) — What to ask any AI writing vendor
