### section-3 (section) — Every sentence is declared, and its strength is measured

> In TextOS, the verifiable units of a draft are its title, its search description, each sentence of the body and each FAQ answer. Each of those units has to be covered by a declared statement, and a unit without one blocks the draft as an undeclared fact. For an editor, this is the missing trail: each sentence can be looked up in the list of declared statements to see what it rests on.
> How strong a sentence is, whether it only describes, asserts an effect or puts a number on something, is assessed by a deterministic rule, and the fact check rather than the writing model has the final say. A sentence that drifts from describing into asserting an effect is judged by that rule, not by how the model labelled it.

### section-4 (section) — What the draft records about itself, and what it does not do

### verify (section) — What to ask any AI writing vendor

> Ask who decides the headings and the purpose of each section, the tool or your team, and whether that outline is fixed before writing starts. Ask what the tool does when a section has no supporting material: does it refuse the section, or write something anyway? Ask whether you can see, for a given sentence, which source it cites, and what happens to a sentence that cites nothing. Ask how the tool judges whether a sentence overstates its source, and whether the model's own label can override that judgement. Ask what the draft records about how it was produced, and whether that record could name a live model run that did not happen. Ask whether drafting and publishing are separate steps, with a person deciding the second.
