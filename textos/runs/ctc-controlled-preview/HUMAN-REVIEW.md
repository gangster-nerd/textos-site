### section-2 (section) — The page you read is the draft that was checked

> The preview step in TextOS no longer picks whichever draft is the latest one. Picking the latest draft is a quiet risk: if another version is saved in between, the page you sign off on may not be the one that was checked. Instead, the preview loads the draft by the identifier written in the handover record and recomputes its fingerprints, with no fallback to the latest version. The comparison stops at the first fingerprint that differs, with no tolerance.
> The fingerprint of the content is computed with its fields in a fixed order, so the same draft gives the same fingerprint after it has been stored and read back. A draft written outside TextOS and imported by hand is rejected if it does not match the brief, the set of evidence or the instructions it was meant for. A mismatch is treated as a reason to stop, not as a detail to smooth over.

### section-4 (section) — What the preview does not decide

> The handover in TextOS carries a draft and its fingerprints from writing to preview, and by its own stated limit it vouches for nothing in the content itself. A matching fingerprint tells you the page was not swapped; it does not tell you the argument is sound or the examples are useful. A draft whose fact-check did not pass, or whose brief was not accepted, never reaches the preview. The preview comes after those checks; it is not a further check of quality.
> Publication is its own state in TextOS, distinct from the preview, so going live remains a separate step after your reading. Whether the article is useful, well argued and worth publishing under your name is still for the person reading the preview to judge.
